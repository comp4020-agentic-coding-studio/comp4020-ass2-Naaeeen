## Event chronology

Our seminars take place in **2035**. The chronology below concerns the earlier
events we study, not the weekly timetable. Where an account gives no precise
calendar date, use its relative period instead of supplying one from memory.

the record. We do not assign an exact ending day here. Accounts that revisit
early Aincrad belong near the beginning, even when we read them in a later week.

## What counts as evidence here?

The assigned readings are **course-compiled case accounts**. They organise
supported events for close reading; they are not separate witness statements.
Their shared preparation matters when accounts agree. The
[source credits](/sources/credits/) give their underlying references and limits.

Keep three things distinct: what an account reports, an explanation you draw
from it, and a response you propose. Classroom maps, hearings and councils are
our analytical work. They do not add new historical events to the case files.

## Use and credit a source

For a required reading, give **After Aincrad course team**, the case-file title,
the account label/title, the paragraph ID, the page URL and your access date.
A short in-text reference can be **(Aincrad case file, B, paragraph B2)**.
Quote these course accounts accurately or paraphrase them with a reference.

You can complete the required work using the accounts on this site. External
source material is available through [Source credits](/sources/credits/) for
checking and further study, without being a hidden preparation requirement.
If you choose to use an additional source, identify it fully and explain its
relationship to your claim.

Ask [Ren Ito](/people/ren-ito/) for an accessible alternative if you need one.
[Course policies](/policies/) explain access, assistance and submission arrangements.
