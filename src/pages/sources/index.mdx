## What counts as evidence here?

The core sources are official synopses, introductions, character guides and
press releases. They document how the works present an event or person. Their
selection and emphasis matter, and agreement between them is not independent
eyewitness corroboration.

Keep three things visible in your notes: what the source states, the explanation
you draw from it, and what your explanation still needs. Our maps, hearings and
classroom scenarios are teaching constructions. Label your own proposals in the
same way. The optional computing readings offer analytical tools, not new facts
about SAO canon.

## Use and credit a source

Follow the specified section before reading further. Record the work, episode
or document, publisher, page or heading, URL and access date. Mark quoted words
as quotations and cite paraphrases too. Use "n.d." when a webpage supplies no
publication date; a copyright footer alone does not date the text you read.

A teaching paraphrase gives you a starting point when access fails. Record that
route and consult the original before quoting it in assessed work. Ask
[Ren Ito](/people/ren-ito/) for a readable alternative if the barrier persists.
[Course policies](/policies/) explain access, assistance and spoiler choices.
