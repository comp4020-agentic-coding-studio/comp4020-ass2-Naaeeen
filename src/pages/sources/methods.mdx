These optional readings connect our cases with computing and design practice.
Use them to frame a question or test an interpretation. They are not accounts
of events in the SAO story, and they add no required viewing or programming work.

## M1. What can a display make possible?

**Source:** Ivan E. Sutherland, *The Ultimate Display*, Proceedings of IFIP
Congress, 1965, pp. 506–508. Read the **first and final paragraphs** in this
[University of Florida text copy](https://cise.ufl.edu/research/lok/teaching/dcvef05/papers/ultimate_display.html).
Allow about five minutes, including notes.

**Teaching paraphrase:** Sutherland imagines computer-mediated environments that
engage more than sight and let people encounter otherwise unfamiliar experiences.
His final imagined display gives computing control over a room's physical
possibilities. It is a speculative vision, not a report of a completed device.

**Try it in Week 1:** place one promised experience beside one form of control
it would require. Compare that pair with NerveGear's fictional promise. Explain
the comparison without claiming that this essay directly caused SAO.

## M2. What should an incident account preserve?

**Source:** John Lunney and Sue Lueder, *Postmortem Culture: Learning from Failure*,
Google's SRE book. Read the introduction's definition of a postmortem and the
first paragraphs under **Google's Postmortem Philosophy** on the
[original chapter page](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/).
Allow about eight minutes.

**Teaching paraphrase:** A useful incident record separates impact, responses,
contributing causes and follow-up actions. Google's account treats review as a
way to improve a system and encourages people to report problems without fear
of blame. Its discussion assumes well-intentioned operators.

**Try it in Week 6 or the Incident study:** use those categories to identify
missing evidence. Then state where the framework's assumptions fit or fail when
coercion or deliberate harm is part of a fictional case. A heading called
"cause" is not evidence that a cause has been established.

## M3. Who can operate the interface?

**Source:** W3C, *XR Accessibility User Requirements*. Read **section 4.4,
Interaction and target customization**, especially User Need 4 and REQ 4a–4d,
on the [W3C document](https://www.w3.org/TR/xaur/). Allow about five minutes.

**Teaching paraphrase:** The guidance asks designers to support people who cannot
rely on precise movements or simultaneous gestures. Large, well-spaced targets
and adaptable inputs can make interaction more usable. It treats a single
assumed body or input method as an insufficient basis for design.

**Try it in Week 7:** choose a barrier in your participation journey and propose
one interface change, one social arrangement and a way to evaluate them. This
guidance helps form the proposal; it does not establish the medical capabilities
of the fictional Medicuboid.
