## Week 1: the promise of Full-Dive

Begin with [Aincrad A1–A2](/sources/aincrad/#a1) and the
[opening notes](/lectures/week-01/). **NerveGear** was the device;
**Full-Dive** was immersive access to a virtual world; **SAO** was the game
entered through it. Ask what entry offered and who could set its conditions.

For our discussions, a *platform* is the organised technical environment within
which people act. *Access* concerns being able to participate; *control* concerns
who can change or refuse the conditions. These are analytical terms we use to
examine the historical case.

## Week 3: craft and meals

<span id="b1" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### B1. Lisbeth's commission

<p id="b1-p1"><strong>B1.1.</strong> Kirito, a player living inside SAO, asked the blacksmith Lisbeth to make him a very high-quality sword. He showed her a rare weapon as the standard he hoped to reach. Lisbeth offered the finest sword she had already made.</p>

<p id="b1-p2"><strong>B1.2.</strong> When Kirito tested that sword's durability, it broke. Lisbeth responded that she could make a sword to satisfy his request if she could obtain a rare metal. The proposed commission therefore needed material that was not immediately available to her.</p>

**Question:** which parts of this task depend on a skilled person, a material
or an agreement? Do not infer a complete economy or a fair price from one commission.

<span id="b2" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### B2. A meal that requires more than an ingredient

<p id="b2-p1"><strong>B2.1.</strong> In October 2024, Kirito obtained rare food while returning from the front lines. He lacked the cooking skill needed to prepare it. At Egil's shop, while trying to sell it, he met Asuna, another player whose cooking skills were advanced.</p>

<p id="b2-p2"><strong>B2.2.</strong> Kirito asked Asuna to cook the food. She agreed in exchange for half of it and invited him to her room because she needed her cooking utensils. An ingredient, learned skill, equipment and agreed terms all contributed to the planned meal.</p>

**Question:** whose work disappears if this becomes only a story about the
person who found the ingredient? A *dependency* is something an activity needs
from another person or resource; an *exchange* is an agreed transfer.

## Week 4: perspective and shared information

<span id="b3" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### B3. Why revisit the beginning?

<p id="b3-p1"><strong>B3.1.</strong> Asuna entered SAO as someone new to online games. Once logout became unavailable, she had to learn an unfamiliar world while already living under its dangerous conditions. Her early experience, recounted in Aincrad C1–C2, places learning and uncertainty at the beginning of the crisis.</p>

<p id="b3-p2"><strong>B3.2.</strong> Kirito had tested SAO before launch, yet his experience did not make tackling the first dungeon alone straightforward, as Aincrad B2 records. The two starting points let us compare unfamiliarity with prior experience without assuming that either describes every player.</p>

This account deliberately brings earlier material together. It is not another
independent witness or a later event. For the Source comparison assessment,
choose any two of the [Aincrad accounts A, B and C](/sources/aincrad/), as
set out in the assessment brief.

<span id="b4" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### B4. Information within a divided group

<p id="b4-p1"><strong>B4.1.</strong> After clearing Aincrad's first floor, Asuna continued with Kirito toward the floors above. Argo supported their progress as an information broker: someone who gathered or supplied information that others could use.</p>

<p id="b4-p2"><strong>B4.2.</strong> Two leading player groups, the Aincrad Liberation Squad and the Dragon Knights Brigade, came into conflict despite their need to cooperate. Their rivalry placed another difficulty alongside the opponents they faced inside the game.</p>

A *guild* is an organised player group. Ask who can obtain information, who can
share it and what happens when groups cannot agree. The information-sharing
charter you draft in class is your proposal, not an additional historical rule.
