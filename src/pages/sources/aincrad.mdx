## Start here

**Sword Art Online (SAO)** was an online role-playing game. A device called
**NerveGear** enabled **Full-Dive**: immersive access to a virtual world.
SAO's setting, **Aincrad**, was a floating castle divided into 100 floors.
Players entered to play, but soon faced conditions that prevented them from leaving.

A examines the conditions announced at launch. B follows the first month of
captivity. C returns to the opening through Asuna's experience as a newcomer.
C is an account of an earlier moment, not an event that happened after B.

## Required reading

<span id="a-the-launch-announcement" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### A. Launch conditions

<p id="a1"><strong>A1.</strong> In 2022, NerveGear made immersive entry into virtual worlds possible. On SAO's launch day, a player known as Kirito entered its world. He and the other players had joined an online game, but its developer, Akihiko Kayaba, announced conditions that transformed their situation.</p>

<p id="a2"><strong>A2.</strong> Kayaba told the players that they could not escape unless the game's levels were cleared. He also announced that dying in the game would mean dying outside it. These conditions tied the challenge inside SAO to the players' lives beyond the game.</p>

**Focus:** what could the operator decide, and what remained within a player's
control? A records the announced conditions; it does not establish what every
player knew before entering or how each person responded.

<span id="b-a-month-into-the-crisis" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### B. The first month

<p id="b1"><strong>B1.</strong> By December 2022, one month had passed since the death game began. Two thousand players had died, and no one had cleared even the first of Aincrad's 100 floors. Progress toward escape remained blocked while the losses grew.</p>

<p id="b2"><strong>B2.</strong> Kirito had been a beta tester: he had tried the game before its public launch. He drew on that experience to tackle the dungeon alone, but the attempt proved difficult. Another player, Diabel, called a meeting to organise a raid on the first-floor boss, a powerful opponent. Kirito attended and teamed up with another solo player.</p>

**Focus:** compare familiarity with a system and the ability to act effectively
within it. B2 records both a difficulty and an attempt at coordination. It does
not establish that every group, raid or experienced player was safe.

<span id="c-revisiting-the-opening-through-asuna" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### C. Asuna's entry

<p id="c1"><strong>C1.</strong> Asuna Yuuki was a school student who had never played an online game before she put on a NerveGear. SAO officially launched on 6 November 2022. The excitement of entry was interrupted when the players discovered that the Game Master had removed their ability to log out.</p>

<p id="c2"><strong>C2.</strong> The players were warned that death in the game would also mean death outside it, and panic spread. Although Asuna was still unfamiliar with the world's rules, she set out to make progress through Aincrad. During the early period she encountered Kirito. Her entry therefore began with learning the world while already trapped within it.</p>

**Focus:** what becomes easier to notice when the starting point is a newcomer?
C gives a particular route into the crisis. It does not tell us that every new
player acted or felt the same way.

## A short glossary

- **Full-Dive:** immersive access through a device to a virtual world.
- **Beta tester:** someone who tries a game before its public launch.
- **Raid:** a coordinated group attempt at a difficult game challenge.
- **Boss:** a powerful game opponent that blocks progress in a challenge.
- **Account:** an organised description of events. These accounts were compiled
  by the course, rather than recovered as separate witness statements.
- **Inference:** an explanation you draw from what an account reports.
