Use this packet for [Week 2: The SAO incident](/sessions/02-the-sao-incident/)
and the [Source comparison assessment](/assessments/source-comparison/).
The required extracts take about 12 minutes to read. Allow another eight
minutes for orientation and notes. No episode, film purchase or prior SAO
knowledge is required.

## Start here

Sword Art Online, or SAO, is the virtual world at the centre of this week's
case. We compare accounts of its opening crisis rather than try to recover
the whole story at once. A and B accompany the television adaptation; C
introduces the later *Progressive* film, *Aria of a Starless Night*.

These are **official promotional summaries of screen adaptations**. Their
selection and emphasis are evidence we can analyse. They are not survivor
testimony or independent witnesses to the events. Keep that distinction in
mind when two texts agree.

A date inside the story and a document's release date do different jobs.
**2022** belongs to this week's event chronology. **29 September 2021** is
when C was published in our world; it does not date the incident.

## Required reading

### A. The launch announcement

**What:** *Sword Art Online*, television episode 1, "The World of Swords";
the official English episode synopsis. Published on the Sword Art Online USA
website; the page supplies no publication date.

**Read:** the short text under **#01 / The World of Swords** on the
[official episode 1 page](https://www.swordart-onlineusa.com/aincrad/story/).
About two minutes. No video is required.

**Teaching paraphrase:** In 2022, NerveGear enables Full-Dive access. At SAO's
launch, the synopsis follows Kirito entering the world. Developer Akihiko
Kayaba announces that escape depends on clearing all levels and that death
in the game means death outside it.

**Why read it:** begin with the rule a public announcement asks players to
accept. Notice whose action structures this account, then ask what it leaves
out about the people receiving it.

**Citation label:** A, episode 1 synopsis, "The World of Swords".

### B. A month into the crisis

**What:** *Sword Art Online*, television episode 2, "Beater"; the official
English episode synopsis on the same website, also undated.

**Read:** the text under **#02 / Beater** on the
[official episode 2 page](https://www.swordart-onlineusa.com/aincrad/story/?no=02).
About two minutes.

**Teaching paraphrase:** By December 2022, a month has passed and 2,000 players
have died. None of Aincrad's 100 floors has been cleared. Despite his beta-test
experience, Kirito struggles to tackle the dungeon alone. Diabel calls a
meeting to organise a group attack on a floor boss.

**Why read it:** compare the scale of the crisis with the actions this short
account chooses to describe. Ask which relationships between events are
stated, and which you would have to infer.

**Citation label:** B, episode 2 synopsis, "Beater", paragraph beginning
"One month".

### C. Revisiting the opening through Asuna

**What:** Aniplex of America and Funimation, press release announcing
*Sword Art Online the Movie -Progressive- Aria of a Starless Night*, dated
**29 September 2021**. This is a film-promotion document, not the film itself.

**Read:** in the [three-page English press release (PDF)](https://aniplexusa.com/pdf/PR_092921_SAO-P.pdf),
read only:

- the paragraph beginning **"Based on the original story"**, from the
  bottom of page 1 into page 2;
- **"Official Synopsis" on page 2**, stopping at "Official Cast".

Allow about eight minutes. Skip the release logistics, cast list and company
profiles. You can use the PDF viewer's text selection or find function to
locate these headings; the document contains selectable English text.

**Teaching paraphrase:** The film revisits the opening through Asuna, who is
new to online games. Its synopsis dates launch to 6 November 2022 and describes
the loss of logout access and the warning of real-world death.

**Why read it:** compare a newcomer's starting point with the television
synopses' emphasis. Describe that change of perspective without silently
combining the adaptations into one account.

**Citation label:** C, pp. 1-2, perspective paragraph; or C, p. 2,
"Official Synopsis". Name the section you actually use.

## A short glossary

- **Aincrad:** the virtual setting named in B and C. For this exercise, you
  only need to know that its floors organise the challenge described in B.
- **Full-Dive:** the immersive form of virtual-world access introduced in A.
  We do not need its engineering details to analyse this packet.
- **Beta tester:** someone who tried a version of a game before its public
  launch. Testing experience may provide familiarity, not knowledge of every
  later condition.
- **Raid:** a coordinated group attempt at a difficult game challenge. In B,
  the meeting concerns a boss, a powerful game opponent.
- **Source:** material used to answer a question. Here the sources are the
  publisher's actual pages and press release.
- **Inference:** an explanation or conclusion you draw from evidence. Label
  it as your reasoning and show the step between the evidence and the claim.
- **Paraphrase:** a restatement in your own words. It still needs a source
  reference; it is not a quotation.

## Make the packet usable

The labelled paraphrases above are brief teaching aids for orientation and
for a temporarily unavailable page. They are not transcripts. For close
reading, use the linked original and record its heading or PDF page.

If the PDF is difficult to use with your reader, its two requested sections
contain selectable text that can be copied into a plain-text document for
reading. For seminar practice, the C paraphrase is enough to start a ledger
row; mark it "paraphrase only". If access remains a problem, raise it in the
seminar before using C in assessed work so an accessible copy can be arranged.

The [lecture's worked example](/lectures/week-02/#from-a-statement-to-an-argument)
shows how to keep a source statement, an inference and a limit apart.
