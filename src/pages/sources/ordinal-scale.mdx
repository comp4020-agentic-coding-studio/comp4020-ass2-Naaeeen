## A different relationship with place

**Full-Dive** let people enter an immersive virtual world. **Augmented reality
(AR)** put digital information and game activity alongside physical surroundings.
**Augma** was the device; **Ordinal Scale** was a game designed for it.
These accounts examine what changed when players remained awake in their surroundings.

## A. From Full-Dive to Augma

<p id="a1"><strong>A1.</strong> NerveGear's Full-Dive arrived in 2022. In 2026, the Augma device offered a different form of access: AR play while users remained awake. Unlike NerveGear and its successor Amusphere, Augma did not provide Full-Dive.</p>

<p id="a2"><strong>A2.</strong> Ordinal Scale became a popular game on Augma. Asuna and her friends were already playing when Kirito joined them. His decision to enter brought him into an activity that the people around him had begun sharing.</p>

**Question:** how does following friends into a game differ from entering a new
system alone? Separate a recorded sequence from an explanation of Kirito's motives.

## B. An interface worn in the world

<p id="b1"><strong>B1.</strong> Augma was a small, headphone-like wearable device. It delivered visual, sound and touch information while its wearer remained awake, rather than moving play into a Full-Dive environment.</p>

<p id="b2"><strong>B2.</strong> Its functions were presented as a way to make fitness and health-management activities game-like. That proposed use describes a purpose for the interface; it is not, on its own, a measurement of safety or an account of every person's ability to use it.</p>

**Question:** which bodily relationship does the interface require? Do not treat
remaining awake as a guarantee of safety or assume one design suits every body.

## C. Place and rank

<p id="c1"><strong>C1.</strong> Ordinal Scale players raised their rank through activities that included collecting items at physical locations and defeating monsters. Rank was an ordered position that determined a player's status within the game.</p>

<p id="c2"><strong>C2.</strong> Higher ranks gave players substantial advantages in player-versus-player contests, often influencing the result of a battle. Access to the same game did not therefore mean access to equal in-game power.</p>

**Question:** distinguish what an interface permits from how a game allocates
advantage. **PvP** is the abbreviation for player versus player.

## Terms and boundaries

The shared-square event used in the seminar is a **classroom proposal**. Its
passer-by, access problem and proposed interface response are not additional
historical events in A–C. Label your design choices and explain which account
gives you a reason to consider them.

These accounts concern the interface, place and ranking. They do not provide a
complete account of risks, personal-data use or accessibility. Carry a specific
remaining question into the [exhibition](/assessments/digital-history-exhibition/)
rather than filling the gap with an unsupported mechanism.
