## How to use this packet

**Kazuto Kirigaya** is the person who uses the name **Kirito** in games.
**Asuna** is another SAO player. **ALO**, or *Alfheim Online*, and **GGO**, or
*Gun Gale Online*, are later online worlds. Returning to a virtual world can
therefore mean entering a different system for a different purpose.

Separate the events an account records from a person's belief at that moment,
your explanation of the events and a response you would propose.

## Week 5: what escape leaves unfinished

<span id="r1" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R1. Return is uneven

<p id="r1-p1"><strong>R1.1.</strong> Kazuto returned from SAO after fighting its creator, Akihiko Kayaba, who had used the name Heathcliff. His fellow survivors also returned, but 300 players, including Asuna, had not regained consciousness.</p>

<p id="r1-p2"><strong>R1.2.</strong> Two months later, Kazuto visited Asuna in hospital. There he met her father, Shouzou Yuuki, and Nobuyuki Sugou, an employee who worked for her father. Leaving the death game had not brought Asuna back to an ordinary life with the people around her.</p>

**Question:** whose needs disappear if "the players returned" becomes the whole
account? A support plan should address relationships and unresolved needs
without inventing a medical diagnosis.

<span id="r2" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R2. A reason to enter another world

<p id="r2-p1"><strong>R2.1.</strong> Egil showed Kazuto a screenshot from ALO containing a girl who resembled Asuna. Kazuto learned that ALO's operator was RECTO, where Sugou worked. These details convinced him that Asuna was trapped inside that world.</p>

<p id="r2-p2"><strong>R2.2.</strong> Kazuto entered ALO using the name Kirito to pursue that possibility. He discovered that his new character retained his SAO data. This account records that retention without explaining the mechanism behind it.</p>

**Question:** distinguish the clue, the conclusion Kazuto drew and the action
he took. Which additional evidence would have tested his belief at that point?

## Week 6: a threat under investigation

<span id="r3" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R3. What is known at the start?

<p id="r3-p1"><strong>R3.1.</strong> A year after SAO had been cleared, the government official Seijiro Kikuoka asked Kazuto to investigate deaths linked to GGO. Two prominent players had been found dead after being shot inside the game by someone using the name Death Gun.</p>

<p id="r3-p2"><strong>R3.2.</strong> Kirito entered GGO despite uncertainty about whether events in that world could directly affect a person's body outside it. Sinon, a sniper he met there, helped him. He entered a major tournament hoping to draw Death Gun's attention and investigate the threat.</p>

**Question:** what would distinguish a direct technical effect from another
explanation of the reported connection? R3 establishes a reason to investigate;
it does not establish a causal mechanism.

<span id="r4" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R4. Acting before the whole explanation is known

<p id="r4-p1"><strong>R4.1.</strong> During the tournament, Death Gun announced that he would cause death outside the game. The announcement was a threat to people beyond their on-screen representations, not simply a claim about winning a match.</p>

<p id="r4-p2"><strong>R4.2.</strong> Kirito and Sinon decided to work together to stop Death Gun before further casualties. They began their pursuit while the full explanation of the deaths remained unresolved in this account.</p>

**Question:** how much evidence is needed to take protective action, and what
would still be needed to establish a cause? Keep your alternative explanations
labelled as hypotheses.

## Week 7: a world worth participating in

<span id="r5" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R5. Making a shared memory

<p id="r5-p1"><strong>R5.1.</strong> Yuuki led a guild called the Sleeping Knights. Its members expected to disband for personal reasons and wanted to create a lasting shared memory. She asked Asuna to help them defeat a floor boss together.</p>

<p id="r5-p2"><strong>R5.2.</strong> They planned to attempt the raid with a single party, although that kind of challenge was normally undertaken by a much larger combined group. Asuna hesitated because of the difficulty of the request.</p>

**Question:** what gives an achievement value when the aim is something a group
can remember together? Do not assign the same circumstances or diagnosis to
every guild member from this account.

<span id="r6" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R6. Access to school

<p id="r6-p1"><strong>R6.1.</strong> Yuuki was terminally ill and could not leave her sterile hospital room. As a tester of the Medicuboid, a medical-use Full-Dive device, she entered virtual worlds from hospital. She wanted to experience school again.</p>

<p id="r6-p2"><strong>R6.2.</strong> Asuna asked Kazuto and friends at school to help make that wish possible. Yuuki was able to take part in a school day. Her participation brought together a means of access, a goal she had expressed and other people's cooperation.</p>

**Question:** which parts depend on technology and which depend on relationships
or decisions? This account of participation does not establish every clinical
capability of the device.

<span id="r7" aria-hidden="true"></span>

### R7. Ordinary days matter

<p id="r7-p1"><strong>R7.1.</strong> Yuuki joined cookouts and quests with other players in ALO. Shared everyday activities became part of her life alongside the group's exceptional challenges.</p>

<p id="r7-p2"><strong>R7.2.</strong> She also participated in classes with Asuna and their friends and joined them on trips to Kyoto. The account places these experiences alongside her activities in ALO, rather than reducing participation to successful access to a system.</p>

**Question:** would counting logins tell us enough about these experiences?
Identify something that such a measure would miss.

## Terms for the discussion

- **Reported connection:** events are linked in an account without a proven
  mechanism explaining the link.
- **Hypothesis:** an explanation to test, not an additional event to insert.
- **Participation:** the opportunity to do something that matters to a person,
  with a meaningful role in deciding how it happens.
- **Support map:** our classroom diagram of a goal, resources, contributions
  and needs that remain unresolved.
