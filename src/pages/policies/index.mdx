Use these arrangements to plan your week, ask for support and prepare assessed work.

## Preparation and workload

Seminars meet on the listed Mondays, **14:00–15:30**, in **Slop Library,
seminar room 2**. Times on this site are Canberra time.

Each week lists a preparation time and a concrete task. Plan for that reading,
a 90-minute seminar and about 30 minutes afterwards to organise your notes.
Reserve additional work blocks for the three assessments; their briefs show
how to start before the deadline.

Start with the [weekly plan](/sessions/), then follow its preparation steps in
order. The assigned case accounts are on this site, free and in English.
Lecture notes are short self-paced readings for selected weeks; their dates
identify the related seminar. Notes assigned in the preparation steps are included
in that allowance. Week 2 uses its notes and slides for the seminar worked example
and later revision. There is one scheduled weekly class. The atlas is optional exploration.

The [case files](/sources/) are sufficient for the assessed tasks. Credits link
the material used to prepare them for anyone who wants to investigate further.
If a required passage is difficult to access, tell the tutor and arrange a
readable copy before quoting it.

## Participation and difficult material

Participation is ungraded. The Source comparison, Incident study and Digital
history exhibition contribute 20%, 30% and 50% of the mark respectively. Source
ledgers, practice writing and peer feedback help you prepare for those tasks.

The material includes confinement, death, coercion and illness. You may step
out, use a written response, or ask
for an alternative passage addressing the same question. No activity requires
you to disclose personal experience. Ask privately if a group discussion or
particular medium makes participation difficult.

Disagree with interpretations by returning to evidence. Give others time to
finish, distinguish a historical actor's choices from a classmate's position, and
make room for a question submitted in writing or through a partner.

## Access and reading choices

The written packets, seminar instructions and readable lecture notes contain
the required work. Slides and moving illustrations offer another way in.
Pause continuous motion or use your device's reduced-motion preference.

Tell [Ren Ito](/people/ren-ito/) when text, contrast, a PDF or a source page is
hard to use. Ask [Mara Vale](/people/mara-vale/) about an alternative format or
adjusted working plan. Describe the barrier and what would help; personal
medical details are not needed for the seminar conversation.

## Handing in work and receiving feedback

Bring the file described in the assessment brief to **Ren's hand-in session,
16:00–17:00 on each assessment's due Friday**, in Slop Library, seminar room 2.
Ren takes a copy, checks that it opens and records your receipt. You may also
hand it in during an earlier seminar. Keep your own copy. If you cannot attend,
someone you nominate may bring your file to the same session. Ren records a
receipt for them to return to you. The same format and marking criteria apply.

## Contact when you cannot attend

Someone you nominate may deliver a sealed written request to Mara at the Monday
seminar or to Ren at a Friday hand-in session. Include your name, the relevant
assessment or week, what you need and a way for Mara to reply privately. Use this
route to request an extension, discuss another delivery method or arrange
feedback collection. You do not need to share personal details with the person
delivering it. If illness or another barrier prevents advance contact, get in
touch as soon as you can.

Source comparison feedback returns in **Week 6, on 26 March**, before you
choose the Incident study case. Incident study feedback returns in **Week 10,
on 23 April**, while the exhibition is taking shape. Collect your written
comments from Ren at the seminar and bring one question to discuss. Arrange a
private collection if you are absent. Exhibition feedback is available at a
final drop-in on **25 May, 16:00–17:00**, in the same room.

## Deadlines and extensions

The assessment pages give deadlines in Canberra time. If work is becoming
unmanageable, speak with Mara in class or request a private consultation,
preferably before the deadline. Bring what you have and propose a realistic
next step. If advance notice was impossible, raise it as soon as you can.

An agreed extension sets a new deadline. There is no automatic late-mark
deduction in this course; agree a completion date with Mara so that feedback
and later work can still be planned. Keep a note of the arrangement. Use peer
feedback to revise, and ask for clarification if you cannot tell what a
criterion asks you to demonstrate.

## Accounts, evidence and classroom proposals

Read the assigned accounts for each week's period. They are course-compiled
secondary accounts with named paragraph locations. Their [source credits](/sources/credits/)
explain how they were prepared. Accounts that share editorial preparation are
not independent corroboration of one another.

Cite the account title, its identifier and the paragraph used. Keep an event's
date and your inference distinct. Label classroom scenarios and proposals as
such; a proposed safeguard or council decision is not a recorded event. Credit
images and your own source-based diagrams, and link optional outside material
precisely if you use it. The assessment briefs give citation examples.

## Tools and assistance

You may use tools for access, language, planning or revision, including AI
assistance. Add a short note to assessed work naming substantial help, what it
changed and which parts it affected. This note is outside the word count.

Check every suggested source and quotation against the original. Keep notes
showing how you reached your interpretation, and be ready to explain it. A
generated account is not evidence of an event. Ask Ren or Mara when you cannot
tell whether assistance has changed the claim you mean to make.
