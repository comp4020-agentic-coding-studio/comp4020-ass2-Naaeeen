## This week's question

**What should a visitor understand differently after the last item in your exhibition?**

Return to our central question: **why do people continue to enter, inhabit and
protect virtual worlds after the SAO incident?** Your answer should explain
connections across the course. A sequence of dramatic events can establish an
order without yet explaining why relationships, institutions or choices changed.

## Prepare in 25 minutes

Bring your current [exhibition draft](/assessments/digital-history-exhibition/):
six curated items across the three course acts and a draft 300-400 word
curatorial argument. Slides, a document and a simple webpage are equally
valid formats. Rough items are useful for feedback; identify anything missing.

Spend **10 minutes** checking account and paragraph references against the
[case files](/sources/), **10 minutes** reading your sequence as a newcomer,
and **5 minutes** naming one question for your reviewer. All required case
accounts are available on the site.

## In the seminar: 90 minutes

- **0–10:** Each person states the exhibition's argument in one sentence.
  Peers identify the proposed connection between its opening and closing items.
- **10–35:** Exchange drafts for a silent walkthrough. Readers record where
  they understood a claim, needed context or could not locate its evidence.
  Authors observe without explaining the missing steps aloud.
- **35–50:** Discuss the observations. Test one bridge between course acts:
  does it explain a change, show continuity or merely place examples together?
- **50–55:** Take a short break.
- **55–75:** Revise one caption and one transition. Check reading order,
  legible text, meaningful link labels and descriptions for informative images.
  A paper or text-only walkthrough can reveal the same explanatory gaps.
- **75–90:** Write a revision plan with three priorities, the evidence each
  needs and a completion order. Recheck the brief before adding new material.

## After the walkthrough

The revision plan is practice work, not another graded submission. Complete the
[Digital history exhibition, worth 50%, by 11 May](/assessments/digital-history-exhibition/)
using the brief's exact requirements. Preserve useful disagreement: a well-supported
history can identify what the available record still cannot settle.
