## This week's question

**What changes when the same early period is told from another perspective?**

Early Aincrad contained newcomers, experienced players and organised groups
called guilds. Argo worked as an information broker, supplying knowledge
that could help others progress. Compare whose experience the accounts
foreground, then ask who could obtain information and decide how to use it.

## Prepare in 25 minutes

Read [B3 and B4](/sources/beginnings/#week-4-perspective-and-shared-information)
for ten minutes. Revisit A and C in the [Aincrad case file](/sources/aincrad/)
for eight minutes. In the final seven, write one comparison and identify an
information holder, someone who could benefit from their knowledge, and a
possible conflict. Mark the last point as an inference or proposal if the
account does not report it.

## In the seminar: 90 minutes

- **0-15:** compare starting points. Which person, activity or difficulty
  becomes easier to notice when the account's emphasis changes?
- **15-30:** map the information relationships in B4. Separate stated
  connections from unanswered questions about trust, access and exchange.
- **30-45:** draft an **invented information-sharing charter**. Agree three
  rules covering who can contribute, who can receive an urgent warning,
  and how a disputed report can be challenged. Attach a reason and source
  location to each rule; label it as a classroom proposal rather than a recorded guild rule.
- **45-50:** take a break.
- **50-65:** another group tests the charter from the position of a
  newcomer and a rival guild. Name one benefit and one possible exclusion.
- **65-80:** revise a rule, then explain in 100 words how the change
  redistributes a power or responsibility. Identify a detail the account
  would need to supply before you could judge the proposal's effectiveness.
- **80-90:** use a partner to check one paragraph of your source comparison
  for a clear claim, traceable evidence and an explained difference.
