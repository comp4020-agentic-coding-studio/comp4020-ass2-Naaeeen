## This week's question

**What changes when a virtual game follows its players into physical places?**

The *Ordinal Scale* introduction contrasts NerveGear's Full-Dive with Augma's
AR play in 2026. Players remain awake, while location and rank organise the
game. This changes our question about returning: joining friends can involve a
different relationship between a body, an interface and its surroundings.

## Prepare in 25 minutes

All reading is free and in English; no device or film viewing is required.
Spend **8 minutes** on the [source packet](/sources/ordinal-scale/), **7 minutes**
on its three linked sections, and **10 minutes** making a comparison with the
[Week 2 account of entry and exit](/lectures/week-02/#choice-is-not-a-single-moment).
Record what each source establishes about bodily presence, leaving and player
advantage. Leave unanswered cells blank. If a page fails, use the packet's
labelled paraphrase and note that access limit.

## In the seminar: 90 minutes

- **0–15:** Compare your interface notes in pairs. Circle one inference that
  initially looked like a source fact. Keep the promotional promise of safety
  separate from demonstrated outcomes.
- **15–35:** Build a shared comparison with four rows: bodily presence,
  location, exit and rank. Add a source location or an explicit gap to each row.
- **35–40:** Take a short break.
- **40–65:** Sketch three frames for an **invented classroom scenario**: an
  Augma-style game event shares a square with a passer-by and someone using a
  mobility aid. Show a conflict, a proposed interface response and its limit.
  This is a design proposal, not an event established by the film page.
- **65–80:** Exchange sketches. Identify whose access or choice improves and
  whose difficulty remains. Revise one frame using the feedback.
- **80–90:** Write an 80-word exhibition caption linking the comparison to a
  reason for entering or returning. Label the proposal and cite the evidence.

## Carry forward

Keep the comparison, sketch and caption as practice material for the
[Digital history exhibition](/assessments/digital-history-exhibition/).
Next week, ask what changes again when the person whose choices concern us is
an inhabitant of a virtual world rather than a visiting player.
