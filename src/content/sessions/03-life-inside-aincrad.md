## This week's question

**How do people make an ordinary life inside an extraordinary crisis?**

A history made only of battles would miss the work that makes a world
inhabitable. This week follows the blacksmith Lisbeth
working on a commission and Asuna preparing a meal. Their contributions
help us examine skill, resources and agreement alongside combat and escape.

## Prepare in 20 minutes

Open [B1 and B2 in the beginnings case file](/sources/beginnings/#week-3-craft-and-meals).
Spend five minutes on the introduction and terms, seven reading the two
accounts, and eight making an inventory. For each case, list what is needed,
who supplies it, and what they expect in return. Use a question mark where
the account gives no answer. All required reading is on the case-file page.

## In the seminar: 90 minutes

- **0-15:** compare inventories. Distinguish possession of an object from
  being able to make use of it.
- **15-35:** draw the dependencies in one case. Include material, skill,
  equipment and agreement only where the evidence supports them. Identify
  one contribution that an account focused on one player might hide.
- **35-45:** begin an **invented exchange exercise** in groups of three.
  One role controls materials, one has the required skill, and one controls
  tools. Negotiate how to combine them and share the result. Record your exercise
  separately from the reported exchanges in Aincrad.
- **45-50:** take a break.
- **50-65:** exchange your agreement with another group. They identify who
  could refuse, which contribution was undervalued, and how the arrangement
  might change if the activity were repeated.
- **65-80:** return to B1 and B2. Write 120 words explaining where the
  exercise helps interpret the sources and where its simplified model fails.
- **80-90:** revise your dependency diagram and share one connection between
  practical cooperation and a relationship that could matter beyond a task.
