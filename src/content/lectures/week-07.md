## Begin with the goal

R5 asks us to notice the value of a shared achievement; R6 gives a wish to
participate in school; R7 makes ordinary activities visible. They help us
ask a historical question that a list of devices cannot settle: what is
this arrangement making possible for the person who uses it?

Begin a participation journey with that person's stated goal. Do not replace
it with a helper's idea of what should be convenient or beneficial. A
technically successful connection might still fail to provide the activity,
relationship or role that made connecting worthwhile.

## Read the technical and social arrangement together

R6 names the Medicuboid in its hospital context and describes school
participation involving other people's help. It does not give the full
technical connection used for the school experience. Keep that mechanism
unspecified. We can still examine the distinction between a means of access,
practical coordination and being welcomed into an activity.

Use four stages: **express a wish, arrange access, join, respond afterwards**.
Beside each, name the person making a decision and the contribution the
source actually identifies. Turn missing arrangements into questions. Who
would handle a change of plan? How would the participant communicate a
preference? What would make taking part feel worthwhile? These questions
extend our analysis; they are not additional source facts.

## Preserve choice when arrangements change

The seminar removes one helper from a classroom scenario to reveal a
dependency. The point is not to assume people are interchangeable. A
replacement could supply practical access while changing the relationship
that mattered. Explain which contribution your revision preserves and
which it cannot reproduce.

Test the revised journey against another case in the packet. A plan that
works for attending a class may not explain the meaning of a guild's shared
achievement. This difference helps prevent "access" from becoming a single
measure that stands in for every kind of participation.

For the [incident study](/assessments/incident-study/), a useful argument
connects a technical condition with a specific human purpose and the work
needed around it. Keep its scale clear: these selected accounts let us
analyse a case, not speak for everyone who might use a virtual environment.
