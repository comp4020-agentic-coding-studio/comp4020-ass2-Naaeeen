## Begin with the goal

Yuuki's cases connect a shared achievement, a wish to attend school and
ordinary activities with friends. Start with what she wanted to do. A list
of devices cannot settle the historical question: what did an arrangement
make possible for the person using it?

In a participation journey, keep that person's stated goal visible. A
helper's idea of convenience or benefit may differ from it. A technically
successful connection might still fail to provide the activity, relationship
or role that made connecting worthwhile.

## Read the technical and social arrangement together

Account R6 places Yuuki's use of the Medicuboid, a medical-use Full-Dive
device, alongside the practical help involved in attending school. It does
not explain the complete technical connection used for that experience.
Keep the mechanism unspecified while examining access, coordination and
being welcomed into an activity.

Use four stages: **express a wish, arrange access, join, respond afterwards**.
Beside each, name the person making a decision and the contribution the
account reports. Turn missing arrangements into questions. Who would handle
a change of plan? How would the participant express a preference? What would
make taking part worthwhile? These questions extend our analysis; they do
not add events to the record.

## Preserve choice when arrangements change

The seminar removes one helper from a classroom scenario to reveal a
dependency. A replacement could supply practical access while changing the
relationship that mattered. Explain which contribution your revision
preserves and which it cannot reproduce.

Test the revised journey against another case. A plan for attending a
class may not explain the meaning of a guild's shared achievement. This
difference helps prevent "access" from becoming one measure that stands
in for every kind of participation.

For the [incident study](/assessments/incident-study/), connect a technical
condition with a specific human purpose and the work needed around it.
Keep the claim's scale clear: these accounts let us analyse a case, not
speak for everyone who used a virtual environment.
