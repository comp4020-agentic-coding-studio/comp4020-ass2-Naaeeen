# The SAO incident

How can we reconstruct a crisis from partial accounts?

<nav className="deck-nav" aria-label="Slide 1 navigation"><span>1 / 10</span><a href="#/sources">Next: the accounts</a></nav>

```notes
Begin in 2035, looking back at the opening crisis. Sword Art Online was a shared virtual world entered through NerveGear, a device that made Full-Dive possible. Aincrad was the floating castle in which its players found themselves unable to leave. The case file introduces this setting and the people involved; no prior knowledge is assumed. We will distinguish entering a world, leaving it and acting within it.

Do not assign an exact day to the meeting: the account supports a month, not a precise date. A chronology should preserve that difference in precision.

Attach an account and paragraph to the first step.

<nav className="deck-nav" aria-label="Slide 4 navigation"><a href="#/clocks">Previous</a><span>4 / 10</span><a href="#/claim">Next</a></nav>

```notes
The seminar guide gives five ledger fields: claim, location, statement, inference and limit. These three steps are its reasoning core. Work through the lecture's example using B2. A limit should name evidence we would need, such as accounts of other players' attempts; it should not dismiss an account without explaining the problem.

## Whose experience sets the starting point?

- Find one shared detail.
- Name a difference in emphasis.
- Explain why that difference matters.

An omission is a question to investigate.

<nav className="deck-nav" aria-label="Slide 7 navigation"><a href="#/revision">Previous</a><span>7 / 10</span><a href="#/choices">Next</a></nav>

```notes
Compare A1–A2 with C1–C2. A directs attention to the rules imposed on players; C starts with Asuna's entry and changing circumstances. The absence of a detail from a short account does not establish that it never occurred. Ask students to explain how emphasis changes their account of the crisis, while recognising that both texts were prepared by the course.
```
