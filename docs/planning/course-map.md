# Course map and teaching progression

Gate 2 established the Week 2 teaching pattern. Gate 3 now implements this
progression with concise weekly guides and source-checked activities. History is
the organising structure; computing explains consequential choices and relations.

Fictional timetable: Semester 1, 2035; twelve consecutive Monday seminars,
19 February to 7 May. Assessment submission window ends Friday 11 May. These are
our teaching dates, distinct from the chronology of SAO events. The 2035 setting
lets the course look back; it does not invent new canonical events.

| Week / date | Topic | Historical question | Teaching purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 / 2035-02-19 | Entering another world | What did Full-Dive promise its first users? | Opening context; distinguish the fictional historical frame from the works used as sources. |
| 02 / 2035-02-26 | The SAO incident | What changes when a platform removes the possibility of leaving? | Build an event sequence and separate what participants knew from later explanations. |
| 03 / 2035-03-05 | Life inside Aincrad | How do people make an ordinary life inside an extraordinary crisis? | Compare cooperation, trade, friendship and survival; avoid reducing the world to boss fights. |
| 04 / 2035-03-12 | Who gets to tell Aincrad? | What changes when the same early period is told from another perspective? | Compare selected television and Progressive material; source comparison due this week. |
| 05 / 2035-03-19 | Escape and its aftermath | What does escape resolve, and what does it leave unfinished? | Connect the end of Aincrad with the ALO story and continuing relationships. |
| 06 / 2035-03-26 | Phantom Bullet | How do earlier events change the way a later threat is understood? | Use accounts and evidence to analyse continuity between virtual and physical consequences. |
| 07 / 2035-04-02 | Mother's Rosario | Who can take part when the boundaries of everyday life change? | Examine access, care and friendship through a selected case, with technical context as needed. |
| 08 / 2035-04-09 | Why return? | When does a virtual place become somewhere a person belongs? | Compare reasons for returning across earlier cases; incident study due this week. |
| 09 / 2035-04-16 | Ordinal Scale | What changes when a virtual game follows its players into physical places? | Contrast AR with Full-Dive and examine location-based play and ranking in the supplied official account. |
| 10 / 2035-04-23 | Artificial lives | Who gets recognised as a person in a designed world? | Introduce Alicization and compare institutional definitions with the experiences the story depicts. |
| 11 / 2035-04-30 | The Underworld conflict | Who has the power to decide a virtual world's future? | Follow the conflict and compare the stakes for residents and outside participants. |
| 12 / 2035-05-07 | After Aincrad | Which history explains why people keep building and protecting these worlds? | Present a small evidence-based exhibition and challenge the course's central argument. |

## Assessment progression

| Assessment | Due date | Weight | Relationship to the course |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source comparison | Friday 16 March, week 4 | 20% | Compare two identifiable accounts of the early Aincrad period; name the adaptation and support the comparison. |
| Incident study | Friday 13 April, week 8 | 30% | Explain one technological and social consequence using the methods practised in weeks 1–8. |
| Digital history exhibition | Friday 11 May, week 12 | 50% | Curate a small set of sources into an argument answering the central course question; slides or a simple webpage are both valid. |

Week 2 was selected and completed as the representative unit. The source
comparison now has a full brief. Gate 3 completes the other assessments and varies
the weekly outputs: control map, exchange diagram, information-sharing charter,
recovery plan, hypothesis board, participation journey, comparative thesis,
interface comparison, recognition hearing, stakeholder council and exhibition.

Twelve dated weeks are required by A2; twelve full lectures/decks are not. Keep
preparation accessible through short official English sources and course-authored
orientation. Selected lecture notes add depth; the Week 2 deck remains the
required working example. Decisions about additional media/decks belong to
teaching quality, not an invented requirement.
